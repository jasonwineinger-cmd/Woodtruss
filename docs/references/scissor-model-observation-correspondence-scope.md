# Scissor model-to-observation correspondence: review scope

This comparison binds a fresh model replay and an independently declared test protocol to one exact model revision, specimen ID and test ID. The declaration must independently identify the observation source and the modeled restraint state it purports to match. A reference string, receipt or equality check does not authenticate the lab, its data, or the correspondence claim.

Before comparing values, require agreement on geometry and restraint-state identity, ordered vertical gauges tied to the actual modeled placements at `Q1`, `R`, and `Q3`, and the identity, position and measurement direction of both bearing channels `L` and `E`. Do not reorder gauges to get a better match or exchange end reactions based on symmetry. Use the same observation sign conventions, units, and load window endpoints and reference convention. In particular, do not compare an absolute reading at one step with a change over a different test interval or with a fitted stiffness slope.

Compare each signed gauge displacement and each vertical bearing reaction as its own row; compare their corresponding gauge mean and reaction total separately. Keep the reference, actual, unit, exact channel identity, and caller-declared diagnostic tolerance. Only surface signed residuals after the protocol and all required individual and aggregate comparisons have been checked; equal sums can mask exchanged, offsetting, or otherwise wrong channels. A match within the supplied tolerance is a diagnostic result for those rows under that declaration. It is not acceptance of the model, evidence that the tolerance is appropriate, or permission to fit model terms, gauge correspondence, or restraint stiffness to the observations.

The existing observed-data reader still checks its selected loading window and regression as part of the observed replay; this comparison neither returns that fit nor adjusts any model parameters.

The historical [USDA FPL-RP-499 scissors-truss results](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplrp/fplrp499.pdf) remain held for correspondence. Their measured load-deflection regression, physical gauges and laboratory restraints are not established as equivalent to the modeled three-gauge and two-bearing outputs. Do not use these historical tables as an automated acceptance target. No serviceability, capacity, connection design, site or code suitability, fabrication, or release follows from this comparison.
