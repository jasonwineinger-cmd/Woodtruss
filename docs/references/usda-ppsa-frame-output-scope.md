## Member end actions: Table VIII and Figure 7 (p. 17)

Use the directed negative-to-positive member endpoints from Tables II and III (p. 13), not the order of any solver export. For directed vector `(dx,dy)`, set `L=hypot(dx,dy)`, `c=dx/L`, `s=dy/L`. Table VIII axial `N` is positive along the directed member, and shear `V` is positive toward member-local up (90° counterclockwise from that direction). Transform a printed end action as `Fx=c*N-s*V; Fy=s*N+c*V`. Positive end moment means counterclockwise and remains unchanged in magnitude under the coordinate rotation. The signs here describe actions on the member, rather than actions exerted by it on the joint; confirm a receiving solver's conventions before comparing. At node 4, the two positive-end rows (members 3 and 4) sum to approximately `(-0.0008,-128.8001,-1657.691)` in global lb, lb, lb-in, matching the external nodal action in Table VI-C within the displayed precision. The forces these members exert on the joint have the opposite signs.

For member 3 (node 3 → 4, direction `(70.250,-23.420)` in), the printed local triples `(N,V,M)` are `(+196.611,+150.992,+1506.153)` and `(-307.700,+182.228,-2662.691)`, in lb, lb, lb-in. Rotating the printed forces gives approximately `(+234.2729,+81.0597)` and `(-234.2728,+270.1899)` lb. Their summed vertical end force is +351.2497 lb. With the projected vertical resultant from the input note, member moment balance about node 3 also closes to rounding. Keep Table VIII's three-decimal original values; Figure 7 is a schematic and its left end moment label differs slightly from Table VIII.

## Nodal displacements: Table XII (p. 20)

Translations are ordinarily global inches (+right, +up), and rotation is in radians (+counterclockwise). For example node 3 prints `(-0.002,-0.027,-0.000294)` and node 4 `(+0.005,+0.001,-0.000510)`. For a member-local motion comparison at these nodes, transform a global translation as `u=c*ux+s*uy; v=-s*ux+c*uy`; the nodal rotation is unchanged by this proper rotation of coordinates. Do not confuse relative local motion of the two ends with the maximum along-member deflection in Table XI: the report distinguishes its member-deflection computation from nodal displacement, which includes shear influence. Figure 8 is exaggerated and illustrates the two quantities separately.

- **Inclined roller, node 5:** Table IV and p. 10 give the free direction `(12,2)`, not its normal. A Table XII footnote specifies that along-roller motion is reported in the column headed horizontal. Therefore the printed row `(+0.004,0.000,-0.000530)` is not a global `ux,uy,theta` triple. With constrained normal motion zero, the inferred global translation from the rounded tangent scalar is approximately `(+0.003946,+0.000658)` in. The independent reaction check is exact at displayed precision: the Table VII reaction `(-62.705,+376.230)` lb has zero dot product with the free direction.
- **Vertical roller, node 2:** the stated direction is `(0,1)` and Table VII shows only horizontal reaction. Yet Table XII prints `(0.000,-0.002,-0.00112)`, with motion in the vertical column, not the footnote's horizontal column. It is compatible with globally vertical motion, but contradicts applying the footnote literally to every roller. Preserve the printed row and document the reading; do not transpose it, turn it into zero, or assert a uniform legacy output rule without replay.
- **Rounding:** the printed translation step is 0.001 in; member force/moment digits are displayed to 0.001 lb/lb-in. If rounded to nearest, half a last-place unit is a display-only uncertainty, not an acceptance tolerance. For node 5 that means a tangent interval of about `[0.0035,0.0045]` in, jointly yielding `ux≈[0.003452,0.004439]` and `uy≈[0.000575,0.000740]` in, both coupled to the same scalar. Rotation precision follows the printed mantissa and exponent (e.g. a 0.000001 rad step at node 5), not the translation step. Extra digits in these conversions are arithmetic only.

## Hold before numerical comparison

The present authored-model analysis path uses a left-pin/right-vertical-reaction idealization, not this example's pin, vertical-travel roller and inclined-travel roller together. An axial truss model cannot reproduce the table's bending/end rotations. A comparison needs explicitly modeled support direction, member end fixity and moment actions, local/global mappings, load convention, and sensitivity to shear and printed rounding. No original executable/deck replay, unsupported legacy-analysis/code checks, inferred physical member/fastener geometry, or manufacturing exports are validated by this note.
