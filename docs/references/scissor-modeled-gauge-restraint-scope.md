# Scissor modeled gauge comparison under horizontal restraint

This is a linear model diagnostic on one directed, level-bearing scissor frame. Use the same geometry, member properties, release assumptions, authored load state, and ideal L/E supports for the baseline and restrained replay. The restrained replay comes from the separately declared horizontal-restraint sensitivity, not a change to either base support flag. Reject an incomplete or failed solve instead of substituting zero motion.

Select the authored upper-chord joints `Q1`, `R`, and `Q3` by exact ID and confirm their modeled placements before comparing: left quarter point, ridge, and right quarter point, respectively, for this topology. Read signed global-Y translations in inches from both solves at those exact joints. Do not substitute a nearby member section, an opposite chord point, a peak displacement or a local member/chord-relative motion. Keep the three readings and their differences. Use only the same equal-weight arithmetic mean in both states: `(yQ1 + yR + yQ3)/3`. Do not fit weights or treat the mean as a single-node reading. Downward global-Y movement is negative; the downward-positive projection is the negative of this mean. Subtract baseline from restrained in the reported convention.

Keep physical reaction signs in the ledger. A downward applied vertical force has a negative Y component; an upward support reaction is positive. The signed added restraint action is horizontal and is shown separately from reactions at the unchanged base constraints. Include that action exactly once in force and moment equilibrium. A change in projected motion need not imply a change in net vertical reaction and does not quantify contact or attachment stiffness.

The [USDA FPL-RP-499](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplrp/fplrp499.pdf) stiffness slopes use measured reactions and an averaged measured deflection under a laboratory setup. This study does not fit or assert a stiffness slope, map Q1/R/Q3 to the report's actual instruments, reproduce specimen geometry, material properties, load intervals, or bearing/rig restraint, or establish any result for a tested specimen. It does not evaluate allowable deflection, compliance, capacity, site suitability, connection behavior, or fabrication.
